Research

To keep it super brief, I want aliens! I want to contribute to the search for life on planets around our own star and others, and hope that extraterrestrial life is found within my lifetime.

To narrow down potential targets in the search for life, we have to understand which conditions could support it. We also have to push our understanding of what “life” can look like to the extremes - building a search around Earth-based expectations could leave us blind to existing alternatives.

And this is super complicated! There’s so much to understand about each aspect of the system: what energy a star supplies, what materials and conditions a planet provides, and how molecules can organise into something living. Each raises complicated questions in its own right, and my research has taken me into all three areas.

But what excites me most is bringing those pieces together. Organisms don’t exist in isolation: they are shaped by the energy available to them, their surroundings, and the history of that environment - the star-planet-life connection. I want to understand how systems with such different scales, compositions, and lifetimes influence one another, what complex molecules they can create, and how these molecules could evolve into living structures.

My projects explore both the individual processes and the connections between them, from stellar chemistry to the possibilities for life in unfamiliar environments. You can find out more about each project below.

Stars

Into the Hearts of Massive Stars

Carbon and Nitrogen Abundances as Tracers of CNO Processing

Through intense radiation, powerful winds, and supernova explosions, massive stars transform the galaxies around them. Their light lets us measure their surface properties, but usually leaves their interiors hidden from view. In certain binary systems, a companion can pull away a star’s outer layers, exposing material once buried deep inside. These “stripped stars” offer a rare opportunity to investigate what nuclear burning has left behind. I study the spectra of a population identified in the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds by Maria Drout, Ylva Götberg and collaborators in 2023. By measuring their temperatures and surface carbon and nitrogen abundances, I test predictions of how massive stars burn their fuel and evolve towards the end of their lives.
